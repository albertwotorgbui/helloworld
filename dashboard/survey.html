Demographics Data Points & Insights

Detailed mapping and explanation of survey sample attributes

📍 Tri-Center Congestion Core

Three stations represent 28.0% of the entire national sample: Kumasi (10% - 296), Weija (9% - 257), and Accra Regional Bohye (9% - 251).
Operational Insight: Frontline workload stress, delays, and queuing times are highly concentrated in these metropolitan centers, highlighting the need for immediate staff augmentation and queue-management automation.

⚖️ Gender Disparity & Vehicle Ownership

The survey reveals a stark gender imbalance: 78.9% Male (2,315) vs. 21.1% Female (619).
Socio-Economic Insight: This imbalance points to underlying demographic divides in vehicle ownership, commercial driving licenses, and access to vehicle finance in Ghana. It indicates a clear research pathway for the RBDI team to design gender-inclusive service campaigns.

👥 Economic Age Driver Segment

Middle-aged adults aged 28-47 represent 66.0% (1,929) of the sample, with the 28-37 cohort constituting the largest share at 40.0% (1,174).
Policy Insight: The overwhelming majority of visitors are in their peak economic and productive years. System inefficiencies at DVLA have direct economic costs by keeping productive citizens away from their jobs and businesses.

💼 Self-Employment Dominance

Over half of the respondents are Self-Employed (52.4% - 1,538), followed by Formally Employed (36.5% - 1,072) and Not Employed (9.8% - 287).
Workforce Insight: The self-employed group represents a critical business demographic (e.g. taxi/ride-hailing drivers, freight operators, traders). Delays directly hit their daily income, which fuels their higher likelihood to use middlemen to speed up transactions.

Channels & Services Insights

Analysis of service delivery bottlenecks and digital pathways

🚪 The 88% In-Person Congestion Trap

An overwhelming 87.7% (2,487) of contacts occur via physical office visits. Digital channels (Website: 5.4%, Call Center: 2.4%, Online App: 2.4%, Social Media: 1.8%, Email: 0.4%) account for a combined total of only 12.3%.
Strategic Bottleneck: Although the online systems are functional and rated 83% responsive by those who use them, a massive trust or accessibility barrier blocks digital migration, forcing congestion onto physical counters.

📄 Licensing Service Load

Driver Licensing represents 76.1% (2,160) of recent contacts, while Vehicle Registration represents 23.9% (677).
Within licensing, Renewals (38.8% - 819) and New Issuances (30.9% - 652) make up nearly 70% of transactions.
Re-design Insight: Renewal is a highly predictable, mechanical process. Setting up automated, self-service kiosks at local stations or malls could instantly migrate 38.8% of physical licensing traffic off the main floors.

🚗 Vehicle Registrations & Inspections

Vehicle-related services are led by Vehicle Registration (48.5% - 634) and Roadworthy Certification (30.2% - 395), followed by Change of Ownership (9.5% - 124).
Quality Control Insight: Roadworthy certifications require physical vehicle inspection. However, qualitative comments reveal concerns about manual inspection bottlenecks and receipt mismatches, pointing to a need for computerized inspection lanes.

🚛 Mobility Profile & Commercial Impact

66.6% (1,924) of visitors drive private cars or SUVs, while 22.2% (642) ride motorcycles. Additionally, vehicle usage is heavily skewed towards Commercial business (36.1% - 1,036) and Private business (32.0% - 920).
Economic Insight: The high proportion of commercial vehicle and motorcycle operators highlights how licensing speed directly affects transport networks and delivery ecosystems in cities like Kumasi and Accra.

Satisfaction & Service Integrity Insights

Analysis of service paradoxes, delays, and integrity gaps

⚡ The Premium Satisfaction Paradox (Value Leak)

Although 40.0% opt for Premium and 17.0% select Prestige same-day services, Premium has the lowest satisfaction rate (80.8%) compared to standard Regular service (84.9%) and Prestige same-day (87.9%).
Unimagined Insight: Customers paying extra for Premium expect faster service. However, due to administrative bottlenecks, Premium processing is often delayed. This mismatch of expectations leads to a critical value leak and customer frustration.

⏳ The Time-Dissatisfaction Connection

While 78.2% complete transactions within 3 hours, 21.8% spend over 3 hours (including 9.1% who spend more than one day). Delays are cited as the primary cause of dissatisfaction by 79.9% (570).
Operational Rule: There is a strong relationship between time spent at an office and customer dissatisfaction. Any transaction taking more than 3 hours drops satisfaction levels significantly, making waiting time the single most critical performance indicator.

💸 Delays as a Driver for Corruption

Qualitative feedback repeatedly highlights issues with extortion, overcharging, and the use of middlemen ("Goro Boys").
Socio-Systemic Insight: Corrupt practices are directly linked to system delays. When standard channels are slow or complex, customers pay middleman fees out of desperation to bypass lines. Reducing transaction times is the most effective way to eliminate "Goro Boys".

🛠️ Hardware & Card Quality Deficiencies

A major theme in qualitative comments is that **issued licenses fade and crack quickly**, showing poor print quality or material durability.
Supply Chain Insight: This points to a materials quality issue. It creates high reprint costs and degrades the DVLA brand. A procurement and supply-chain quality audit is highly recommended.
